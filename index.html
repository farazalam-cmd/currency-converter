The formula

All rates on this page are stored as units per 1 US dollar. A conversion goes through dollars in one step:

result = amount ÷ rate(from) × rate(to)

Example: 100 USD to INR is 100 ÷ 1 × 94.928524 = 9,492.85 INR at the snapshot rate.

Frequently asked questions

Are these rates live?

They are a dated snapshot, not a live feed: the rates published by ExchangeRate-API on 3 November 2026 at 00:02 UTC (5:32 AM IST). Exchange rates move constantly while markets are open, so for a large or time-sensitive transfer, check a live quote from your bank or transfer provider at the moment you send.

What is a mid-market rate?

The mid-market rate is the midpoint between the buy and sell prices on the global currency market — the “real” rate with no margin added. It’s the fairest basis for comparison, but it isn’t the rate you’ll actually be offered: banks and transfer services add a spread, and often a fee, on top of it.

Why is the rate my bank offers different?

Banks and money-transfer apps buy currency cheaper than they sell it, and the difference is how they earn. They may also charge a flat or percentage fee, use a rate fixed earlier in the day, or add charges at the receiving end. Always compare the final amount the recipient gets, not just the headline rate.

Which currencies can I convert?

All 166 currencies in the snapshot — major currencies like USD, EUR, GBP, INR, JPY and AED, plus many others from Afghan afghani (AFN) to Zambian kwacha (ZMW). If a currency isn’t in the list, it wasn’t in the source data for this snapshot.

How are amounts rounded?

Results are shown to two decimal places for most currencies, with more places when the value is very small (for example, converting a few rupees into Kuwaiti dinar). The underlying calculation uses the full published rate, so rounding only affects what’s displayed, not the maths behind it.

Does this page store what I type?

No. Amounts and currency choices stay in your browser for this visit only. Nothing is sent to a server, no account is needed, and no cookies or saved data are used to track conversions.

Can I use these figures for business or tax purposes?

Use them as a guide only. For accounting, tax, or legal conversions, use the rate your bank applied on the transaction date, or the official rate your tax authority prescribes — this page is not financial advice.
